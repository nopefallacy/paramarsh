Brand foundations · v0.1 · for review

Look carefully. Say what you saw.

Everything Paramarsh makes, from the website to a field report to a slide in a client meeting, should come from one idea and use one visual language. This page sets both out.

01

The idea

Beyond the surface: reasoning carefully from what can be seen to what lies beneath it, and being honest about the difference.

That sentence is the brand. Every visual and verbal choice below exists to make it felt. The four principles are four ways of practising it: understand the ground (competence), keep the standard (consistency), show the reasoning (transparency), remember who it is for (benevolence).

The name

परामर्श

parāmarśa, from the Sanskrit root mṛś: to touch, to grasp, to consider. In Hindi today it means counsel. In classical Indian logic it is the step of inference between seeing smoke on a hill and knowing there is fire.

The feeling we are after

Being quietly moved.

Not impressed, not sold to. The feeling of suddenly seeing the people who were there all along. Psychologists call it kama muta, Sanskrit for "moved by love": the warmth that comes when we feel connected to others. We earn it slowly, with restraint, and spend it once, at the end.

02

The mark

Four rings and a light. The rings are not perfectly concentric: each centre shifts a little up and to the right, the way contour lines crowd on one side of a hill. It reads as a summit on a map, a cross-section of a tree, a ripple, and a fingerprint, which are the four drawings the brand is built from.

  • Outer ringCompetence. The ground, understood in full before anything is drawn on it.
  • SecondConsistency. The standard that holds ring after ring.
  • ThirdTransparency. The reasoning, open to inspection.
  • FourthBenevolence. Closest to the centre: the people affected.
  • The lightThe person the work is for. Always vermilion by day, ember at night. Never omitted.
  • The driftThe dashed line shows the shift of centres: the slope. Construction only; never part of the mark.

03

One line, four readings

The brand draws with a single thin line. Arranged four ways, it carries the four principles. Each chapter of the website uses one, and so can reports, decks and diagrams.

Contour · Competence

Lines of equal height. How you read a whole landscape from a flat sheet.

Tree ring · Consistency

Wide years and narrow ones. Every ring closes.

Strata · Transparency

A conclusion cut open. Unknown boundaries dashed and marked with a question.

Ripple · Benevolence

A decision spreading outward. Up close, a fingerprint.

04

The honesty rule

Borrowed from cartography and geology, where it has meant the same thing for centuries. It is the one rule that applies to every line Paramarsh draws, in every medium.

Solid means observed.

Measured, verified, documented, approved for publication. A solid line on a chart is data. A solid border on a card is a real case study.

Dashed means inferred.

Projected, estimated, illustrative, hypothetical or not yet known. A dashed line on a chart is a projection. A dashed tag says Illustrative example

This puts the blueprint's non-negotiable editorial rule into the visual system: never present illustration as evidence. Every generated drawing on the website carries a dashed tag, and so should every hypothetical scenario in a report.

05

Paper and five inks

Paramarsh is printed, not rendered. Everything sits on buff survey paper, and colour only ever arrives as an ink with a job, the way a topographic sheet is printed one plate at a time. The paper takes the light of the hour: cool at dawn, bright at noon, golden in the evening, and an indigo night sheet for the ending.

05b

The sheet

Instead of interface furniture, the brand borrows the furniture of a printed map. Each device has a job.

Neatline

A double rule around everything.

It frames the page as one document, with registration marks at the corners. The margin carries the sheet number and the hour.

Cartouche

A titled box set over the map.

Text that sits over imagery goes in one. Always paper, always the double rule.

Legend

Every symbol explained.

Controls are legends: a symbol, a name, and an italic note saying what it shows. Choosing a layer is reading a key.

Glass

Look closer.

The loupe is how we show "beyond the surface". It reveals detail under plain paper wherever the visitor looks.

07

Charts & evidence

Charts are where a development practice is most tempted to overstate. Ours are drawn in the same thin line, follow the honesty rule, and always show their uncertainty and their source.

Illustrative data

Observed, then projected

Solid where measured. Dashed where projected, with the range of uncertainty hatched in vermilion.

Illustrative data

What a recommendation rests on

A strata bar: the share of a decision supported by measurement, by secondary sources, by judgement, and what is unknown.

  • The unknown is always drawn. Never trimmed to make a bar look complete.
  • Hatching, not colour alone, separates categories, so charts survive greyscale print and colour blindness.
08

Motion

Motion is time passing, never decoration. If a movement doesn't tell the reader something, such as that the land has depth, that a ring has closed, or that a light has come on, it is removed.

Pace

A walking speed.

Slow camera, long eases (expo and power3 out), nothing faster than a reader can follow. Scroll drives the story; it never takes control of the scroll.

The press

Ink, plates, register.

Words settle into the paper like ink. A second colour arrives slightly off-register and snaps into place. Layers come apart as plates and go back into register. What is observed is drawn before what is inferred.

Stillness

Earn the climax.

Hold back motion and warmth until the end. The lights come on once. A reduced-motion mode replaces travel with still compositions and keeps every word.

09

Voice

Plain words, short sentences, and a second sentence that goes one layer deeper than the first. We write like a thoughtful field researcher talking to someone they respect: no jargon to hide behind, no superlatives, and limits said out loud.

Instead of

We deliver world-class, end-to-end sustainable development solutions.

Say

Before we act, we understand.

Instead of

Our robust QA framework ensures excellence at every stage.

Say

Anyone can do good work once. The test is the fiftieth time.

Instead of

Data-driven insights you can trust.

Say

Here is what we know, where it came from, and what we don't know yet.

Instead of

Empowering communities for a brighter tomorrow.

Say

Who does this help, and what does it change for them?

The work is never just about what we build. It is about how, why, and who it serves.
